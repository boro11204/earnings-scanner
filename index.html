How it works

The scanner runs every weekday at 11:00 AM ET. It checks every company reporting earnings on the next trading day and looks for stocks where the options are pricing in a bigger move than the stock usually makes. After the report that extra price usually drains out of the options (the "IV crush"), and that is what the trade sells.

A stock has to pass every rule. The first one it fails is the skip reason:

  1. Earnings tomorrow, with an option expiry after the report.
  2. Stock at $20 or more, and at least 500,000 shares traded a day.
  3. Busy options: 300+ contracts traded today in that expiry, or 5,000+ open interest.
  4. Enough history: at least 6 past earnings reports.
  5. Earnings really move it: the average past earnings move is at least 3 normal days' moves.
  6. No wild gappers: the worst past move is no more than 2.2× the average one.
  7. Options look expensive: today's implied earnings move beats last year's move at the same quarter, and is at least as big as the average past move.
  8. This week's options are pricier than next month's (at least 1.3×).
  9. Strike far away: the short strike is at least 1.5× the average past move and 1.2× the implied move from the stock price. If one strike's volatility sits clearly above the curve (a "smile bump"), that strike is sold instead.
  10. One side only: a put spread or a call spread, with the protective option 1–4% further out.
  11. Worth it: the credit is at least 10% of the spread's width at the mid price.
  12. Size: enough contracts for about $1,000 maximum loss. The best 3 a day become trades.

Paper rules: enter at the mid price before the close on the scan day. Close the morning after the report at 9:35 AM ET (cross the spread by 10:00 if needed). No stop, never held to expiry. Commission is $0.50 per contract per leg, paid on the open and again on the close.

P&L on this page is the 9:35 AM exit from the sheet minus those commissions. The scanner only sends signals; it never places an order.
